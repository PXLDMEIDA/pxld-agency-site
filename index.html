01Sound familiar

What promoters tell us before they call.

Every one of these has a fix, and every fix is something we measure in the account, not something we promise on a call.

"I boost posts and get likes, not ticket sales."

Boosts and traffic campaigns find people who tap. We build for people who buy.

"The budget is gone before the final week."

Flat daily budgets spend evenly while most tickets sell in the last 72 hours. We ramp toward the date.

"I can't tell which ad actually sold tickets."

The pixel is on the wrong page or missing from checkout. We fix tracking before launch.

"Cold audiences cost a fortune."

Your likeliest buyers already follow you or bought last time. We sell to them first.

"Free RSVP nights make the ads look like they're failing."

A $0 purchase wrecks ROAS when the room is full. We judge each event on the right number.

"The creative feels tired by week two."

We plan announce, push and last-call creative so the feed never sees the same flyer twice.

APPROACH/How we build

Same brands, same creative, same cities. The difference is the build.

Campaigns built to find ticket buyers sell tickets. Campaigns built to get clicks get clicks. Here is the exact process every event goes through, from the audit to the last-call push.

01

Audit

Before any spend

We check the plumbing first. Pixel on the live checkout, the right ID in the right place, parallel campaigns bidding against each other, and where last event's budget actually went.

0.90x → 4.63x
A failing campaign rebuilt on the sales structure, turned around inside 9 days.
02

Build

Announce week

One build per event, split by objective on purpose. A purchase-optimised sales campaign carries the budget. Warm audiences of engagers, viewers and past buyers go first, with warm excluded from cold so the sets never compete.

8.77x vs 2.54x
Same event, same ticket page: purchase-optimised build vs traffic build.
03

Pace

On-sale to event day

Budget ramps toward the date and stops on it. Lifetime budgets tied to the event, reach front-loaded to build the warm pool, sales spend concentrated in the final 72 hours when most tickets move.

7.31x
Event-day return on one account, against 1.88x at 8 to 14 days out.
04

Optimise

Daily

Judged on cost per ticket, never CTR. Creative rotated before it fatigues, weak ad sets cut, spend moved to what's selling. Free-entry nights are judged on cost per RSVP so the report reflects the real goal.

$1.86
Cost per RSVP on a free-entry night, 322 sign-ups, judged on the right KPI.
05

Report and repeat

After the event

A one-page report and a playbook for the next one. Tickets, revenue, cost per ticket and which creative won. Every buyer becomes next event's warm audience, so each event starts cheaper than the last.

2x to 3.5x
How much cheaper warm audiences of past buyers convert than cold ones.
RULESWhat we stopped doing

Four habits that quietly burn ticket budgets.

Boosting posts
Sales campaigns on the checkout

Boosts optimise for engagement. Meta then finds people who like things, not people who buy tickets.

Flat daily budgets
Lifetime budgets that ramp

Even spend means the campaign is starved exactly when buyers are ready to commit.

Cold audiences first
Warm stacks first

Your followers and past buyers convert at a fraction of the cost. Cold scales what warm proves.

Reporting on clicks
One number: cost per ticket

Cheap clicks with an empty guest list is not a result. We report what the room cares about.
